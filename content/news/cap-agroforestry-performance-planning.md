Finding a policy objective that mentions resilience is not the same as identifying support available to a particular farm. An agroforestry proposal needs to connect the applicable intervention, the farmer's circumstances and the work that will actually be delivered. It also needs to explain how the project remains manageable when a payment arrives later than expected or covers only part of the cost.

## Start with the national implementation, not an EU-wide assumption

The European Commission explains that the 2023–27 Common Agricultural Policy is implemented through national CAP Strategic Plans, with separate plans for Flanders and Wallonia in Belgium. The plans and amendments are approved by the Commission. Annual performance reports are used to monitor implementation, including spending, outputs and results.

The CAP performance monitoring and evaluation framework uses common indicators to examine performance and inform assessment. That programme-level structure should not be mistaken for proof of a particular farm's environmental or financial outcome. Nor does a general CAP objective establish the eligibility of every tree-based farming proposal.

The rest of this article offers FarmTree's practical interpretation for project preparation. Specific eligibility, application and payment questions belong with the current national or regional rules and the responsible authority.

## Build an intervention sheet for the actual proposal

For each candidate support route, record the intervention name, current source, administering body and the activities being considered. Add the eligibility questions that remain unresolved. Do not merge several possible measures into a single assumed funding rate before those questions have been answered.

The sheet should distinguish an official condition from an internal assumption. If the team expects an activity to qualify but has not confirmed it, label that expectation accordingly. If the source is an older call or a superseded plan, do not carry its terms forward without checking the current material.

Give the farmer a usable summary of the obligations as well as the potential support. A project can look attractive in a grant table while creating a management commitment that does not fit the farm. Ask who will carry out each activity, how it interacts with existing work and what happens if the proposed schedule changes.

## Keep support payments separate from operating performance

In a scenario comparison, show the farm's operating costs and receipts separately from assumed support. This makes it possible to see whether the intervention changes the underlying production system or mainly changes its financing. Both questions matter, but combining them can obscure the source of an apparent improvement.

Record payment timing explicitly. A contribution towards establishment does not necessarily eliminate the need to finance expenditure before the payment is received. Similarly, a recurring payment should not be assumed beyond the period supported by the applicable rules or agreement.

Our recommendation is to compare at least the proposed support assumption with a delayed or reduced-support case where that uncertainty is relevant. These are planning scenarios, not predictions about an authority's conduct. Their purpose is to identify the financing exposure the farm or programme would need to manage.

## Decide which observations support which conclusion

An administrative record can show that an activity was delivered. It may not answer whether the activity produced the intended change in soil, habitat or resilience. Make the distinction explicit when building the monitoring plan and the reporting language.

For example, a record of an agreed management area and a record of observed crop performance answer different questions. Do not relabel the first as proof of the second. Where the programme requires particular indicators or reporting fields, follow those requirements while keeping additional project-learning questions identifiable.

Avoid collecting everything merely because a digital form allows it. Link each observation to a requirement, a management decision or a defined evaluation question. Assign a person to review the information and describe how the result could change the plan. Data without a use can become a continuing cost without improving implementation.

## Account for labour and coordination

Agroforestry proposals often involve work by several parties: the farm, advisers, contractors and programme administrators. Build a shared calendar of establishment, management, evidence collection and reporting. The calendar should reveal conflicts with existing farm operations rather than assume that new work can always be added.

Ask which responsibilities continue after the initial support application. A proposal that budgets for advice at the start but not for later review may rely on capacity that nobody has agreed to provide. Record the handovers and the information each party needs to complete its work.

The [FarmTree Platform](/platform/) can support structured scenario comparison, while the [Farm Valuation discussion](/agroforestry-farm-valuation-timing/) examines timing and cost assumptions in more detail. Neither a model result nor a favourable scenario replaces the applicable funding decision or the farmer's assessment of feasibility.

## Review amendments without rewriting history

CAP plans and implementation arrangements can evolve. Keep a dated copy or reference for the terms used in a project decision and establish when they need to be checked again. If a later amendment changes an assumption, preserve the earlier decision record and document the consequence for the proposal.

This is particularly important when several farms enter a programme at different times. Do not assume that the conditions assessed for the first participant automatically apply to everyone joining later. A clear source-and-date field is a small administrative step that can prevent a much larger misunderstanding.

The practical takeaway is a three-part decision file: the verified intervention conditions, the farm-level implementation and cash-flow scenario, and the evidence plan. Keeping those parts connected but distinct makes the proposal easier to assess without overstating either support certainty or environmental performance.
