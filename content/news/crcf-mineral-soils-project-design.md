An agroforestry project can be physically well designed and still be poorly prepared for an external assessment. The missing element is often not another model run. It is a clear account of what will be observed, who will maintain the records and how evidence will be connected to the activity being assessed. Carbon-farming policy makes that operational design a first-order planning question.

## A methodology milestone, not automatic certification

The European Commission reports that it adopted carbon-farming certification methodologies in July 2026 covering agriculture and agroforestry on mineral soils, peatland and other organic-soil restoration, and afforestation. Its implementation page separately discusses entry into force, recognition of certification schemes and assessment of models or proxy-based frameworks. Those stages should not be collapsed into a statement that an individual farm, software tool or proposed project is already certified.

For project teams, the immediate implication is preparatory rather than promotional: identify the intended route and the evidence it will require before promising a certified result. The recommendations in this article are FarmTree's planning interpretation of that policy direction. They are not a clause-by-clause eligibility assessment or a claim of FarmTree recognition.

## Draw the activity boundary before calculating the benefit

Start by describing the land and management changes under consideration. Which plots are included? Which activities will change? Which parts of a wider farm remain outside the proposed assessment? Record the answers in a way that can be connected to field records, rather than relying on a project name that means different things to different partners.

A boundary is also a practical coordination device. The agronomist, financial analyst and monitoring specialist should know whether their work concerns the same area and management schedule. If one scenario includes future expansion and another includes only the first planting phase, apparent differences between their results may come from scope rather than performance.

Create a short boundary note alongside the map. Explain exclusions, unresolved ownership questions and assumptions about future participation. Do not hide those qualifications in a final report appendix after the investment decision has already been made. A smaller, clearly defined first phase can provide a more reviewable starting point than an ambitious area estimate with uncertain participation.

## Separate the starting condition from the proposed intervention

Project records should distinguish observations about the current system from assumptions about the proposed system. Existing tree cover, current management and planned planting are not interchangeable inputs. Nor should a modelled starting value silently replace a field observation without a record of the decision.

Our recommended working format is a paired evidence sheet. One side records the starting condition and its source; the other records the proposed activity and the person responsible for implementing it. Add a third field for uncertainty or missing information. This is an internal planning tool, not a substitute for the baseline definition required by the applicable certification methodology.

Use the sheet to identify assumptions that could change the project decision. A small uncertainty in a low-cost administrative item may be less consequential than uncertainty about establishment, management capacity or the area actually available. Prioritise new observations according to the decisions they can improve, while meeting any specific methodological requirements separately.

## Budget for an evidence system, not only for planting

A monitoring plan needs people, time and continuity. Assign responsibility for maintaining plot identifiers, recording management changes, reviewing data quality and responding to queries. If those duties are left as an unnamed future service, the project budget may describe the physical intervention more completely than the evidence needed to assess it.

Consider staff turnover and partner handovers. A reviewer joining later should be able to locate the current protocol, understand how it changed and identify the records associated with each version. Store decisions alongside the relevant data rather than expecting institutional memory to bridge the gaps.

The same discipline applies to model outputs. Preserve the input dataset, model version, scenario definition and interpretation of each result used in a decision. The companion article on [model-recognition evidence](/crcf-model-recognition-evidence/) explores why a quantification tool's assessment and a project's evidence remain distinct questions.

## Test whether the plan can survive a management change

An implementation plan should explain what happens when reality departs from the proposed schedule. A farmer may alter a crop combination, delay an operation or replace a management practice. The appropriate technical response depends on the applicable method and project circumstances; the organisational response should not depend on somebody remembering to mention the change months later.

Define a reporting route for material changes and a review responsibility. Preserve the original assumption, record the observed change and identify which scenarios or evidence records require reconsideration. Do not silently revise a past projection so that it appears to have anticipated the new situation.

This is also a useful way to connect financial and environmental planning. The [FarmTree Solutions](/solutions/) context can help teams frame linked planning questions, but the resulting scenarios should remain labelled as projections. A financial assumption about future carbon revenue must not be mistaken for an entitlement to units or a guaranteed payment.

## The next useful deliverable

Before commissioning a larger assessment, assemble a compact evidence-readiness pack: the proposed boundary, starting-condition sources, activity schedule, responsibilities, unresolved questions and intended certification route. Ask the relevant technical advisers to identify the gaps that matter to that route. The objective is not a thicker report; it is a project design whose assumptions and records can be followed by someone outside the original team.
